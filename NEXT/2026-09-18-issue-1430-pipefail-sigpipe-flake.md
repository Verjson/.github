---
date: 2026-09-18
issue: 1430
title: Stop pipefail from reporting grep -q's early exit as a contract violation
impact: patch
---

`grep -q` exits on its first match. Reading from a pipe, that closes the pipe while the
producer is still writing, so the producer dies on `SIGPIPE` and `set -o pipefail` reports
`141` as the pipeline's status. Thirty-nine assertions across eighteen scripts were built on
that shape, so any of them could redden a required check on a pull request whose diff could
not have caused it — `ci-eligibility.test.sh` did exactly that on #1417, reporting that
`node-ci.yml` lacks `statuses: read` when it grants it.

Measured on the reported assertion at 6 concurrent workers: 425 spurious failures in 9000
iterations, every one `141`, against 0 in 9000 after the fix; 0 in 200 unloaded, which is
why it survived so long. Each site now drops `-q` and redirects to `/dev/null`: without
`-q`, `grep` must read to EOF, so the producer never sees `EPIPE`. The exit status and the
assertion it feeds are unchanged.

`scripts/ci-gate/pipefail-sigpipe.test.sh` keeps the shape out. It reproduces the race
deterministically rather than by stress, checks that the remedy still reports a genuine
non-match as `1`, and scans every tracked script under `scripts/` — asserting the
enumeration is non-trivial so an empty scan cannot pass for a clean one.

Three further occurrences in `scripts/gen-changelog-caller.sh` are left alone deliberately:
they are emitted verbatim into the generated adopter artifact `changelog-contract.test.sh`,
so fixing them here changes generated bytes in every adopter and fails
`required-checks-audit.test.sh` with `generated-contract-byte-drift`. Those, and the same
shape inside generated workflow `run:` blocks, belong to the regeneration at a new contract
SHA tracked in #1431. The frozen conformance regression fixtures are untouched.
